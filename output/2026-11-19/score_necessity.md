Top-3 notes: GRID scores 10 because the grid itself is the bottleneck for the whole electrification story: the IEA's World Energy Outlook 2026 puts 25 million km of new power lines needed over the next decade, with grid investment needing to rise about 50 percent by 2030. No substitute exists for moving electricity from generator to user, so this is the one theme that is civilisation-critical in the literal sense. SMH scores 9 because semiconductors are the irreplaceable input to AI and electrification: McKinsey raised its 2030 market forecast 44 percent to USD 2.3 trillion, with data-centre chips rising from USD 330bn in 2025 to USD 1.2tn in 2030. The single point off 10 is cyclicality, not substitutability, since no alternative to chips exists at any scale. REMX scores 9 because rare-earth magnets have no drop-in substitute in EV motors, wind turbines and defence systems: the IEA puts magnet rare-earth demand up 30 percent by 2030, with China making about 94 percent of sintered magnets. The single point off 10 is that recycling and magnet redesign could blunt demand at the margin by 2050, per the same IEA report.
Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Silver: the memo calls it unclear with a 35M oz deficit still forecast; I score 6, not 7, because the Silver Institute's 2026 survey has PV silver demand down 19 percent on thrifting and total industrial demand down 3 percent, so the growth claim is weakening even as the deficit persists.
- Gold: the memo notes central banks bought 321t in Q3 2026, up 42 percent year on year; I score 6, not higher, because World Gold Council total Q3 demand was flat, down 1 percent year on year, with jewellery down 17 percent, so there is no structural growth claim for the metal itself.
- Crypto (spot and equity): the memo calls the drop rotation with no thesis change; on necessity I score spot 4 and miners 3, because demand for both rests on bitcoin holding above USD 70k and on ETF flows, which are conditions that may not hold, not a civilisation need.
- Space: the memo calls it rotation with no theme-specific news; I score 6, not 7, because satellite and launch demand is still pre-scale and discretionary, with no load-bearing need in 5-10y.
- Oil/gas: the memo leans break on a fading war premium and IEA demand softening; on necessity I score 6, because oil and gas still have no substitute at scale in 5-10y for aviation and petrochemicals, so this is cyclical decline risk, not a fashion trade.
