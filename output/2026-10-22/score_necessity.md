**Top-3 notes**

- **XLU (9):** EIA's 2026 STEO projects US electricity use at records in both 2026 and 2027, the first four-year rise since 2007, and it attributes the surge to large computing centres (EIA press release, Jan 2026; Reuters on Jul-2026 STEO: 4,195 bn kWh in 2025 to 4,399 bn kWh in 2027). Electricity has no substitute, so the only reason this is not a 10 is that the growth rate is low single digits.
- **COPX (9):** The IEA's Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 projects copper demand rising from 27 Mt to 34 Mt by 2040 (STEPS) with a ~30% primary-supply shortfall by 2035 from the current mine pipeline (Argus, May 2025). Mines take ~17 years from discovery to output, so supply cannot route around it either; aluminium substitution is the only visible offset.
- **URNM (8):** WNA's World Nuclear Fuel Report 2025 (Sept 2025) has reactor capacity growing from 398 GWe to 746 GWe by 2040 in the reference case, lifting uranium requirements from ~68,920 tU to just over 150,000 tU. Once reactors are built the fuel has no substitute, but new nuclear still competes with gas and renewables plus storage, which keeps it in band 7 +1 rather than band 10.

**Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo**

- **Silver** is not only a "blow-off unwinding" on real yields. The World Silver Survey 2026 has PV silver demand down 19% in 2026 from thrifting and copper substitution, a structural erosion of the main industrial growth driver.
- **Lithium:** the memo blames oversupply and the ALB CEO succession. It misses the substitution vector: CATL sodium-ion mass production starts in Q4 2026, and BNEF expects ~7% of lithium demand to be displaced by 2035.
- **Clean:** the memo finds "no October-specific break" and calls it rotation. On necessity, US demand is structurally reduced: the OBBBA ends wind/solar credits for projects placed in service after 2027-12-31, and IEA cut its 2025-30 renewables forecast ~5% (-248 GW). This hurts QCLN and PBW more than global ICLN.
- **Crypto-equity:** the memo calls this an AI-capex wobble. That fits WGMI, whose miners have >$114bn of AI/HPC contracts, but not BLOK or BKCH, which still rest mostly on crypto prices. BTC production cost (~$80k, Q4 2025) above price is a real necessity weakness for mining itself.
- **ai/robot:** the memo treats six funds as one theme. They hold different sub-themes: AIPO is AI power, BAI is AI semis, QTUM is quantum plus semis, ARKQ is autonomy/Tesla, and BOTZ/ROBO are industrial robots. They are scored 6-8 accordingly.
- **Space:** the memo ties UFO and ARKX to the SpaceX IPO slide. That is a sentiment story; the WEF/McKinsey 9%/yr growth path is intact. ARKX is only partly space (drones, eVTOL, software), so it is scored a point lower.
- **Rare earths:** the memo says the US-China truce deflated the scarcity premium. That may explain price, but necessity is unchanged: magnet rare-earth demand is up >30% by 2030 (IEA, Apr 2026), and the DFARS Chinese-magnet ban starts 2027-01-01.
- **Transport:** the memo treats elevated oil as rotation. IATA reportedly cut its 2026 airline profit forecast to ~$23bn on a fuel shock (single source, unconfirmed). Demand is intact; the 3.9% margins are what make JETS band 5.
