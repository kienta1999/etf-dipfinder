## Top-3 notes

- **GRID (10):** The IEA's *Electricity 2026* report (November 2026) says meeting power demand growth will require annual investment in grids to rise by 50% by 2030, with more than 2,500 GW of projects stalled in connection queues worldwide. That queue is the load-bearing fact: the grid is the binding constraint on electrification and AI data centres, and there is no substitute for wires to deliver power.
- **SMH (10):** McKinsey's 2026 revision puts the semiconductor market at $2.3 trillion by 2030, about 44% above its autumn-2025 estimate, driven by five hyperscalers' announced capex of about $800 billion for 2026 and $1 trillion for 2027. That capex wall is the load-bearing fact: chip demand is now infrastructure demand, not a consumer cycle, and no alternative technology can route around silicon at scale.
- **PSI (10):** TSMC's advanced-node capacity is booked through 2028 and it has told Nvidia and Broadcom it cannot meet all of their AI chip demand, with CoWoS packaging sold out and lead times stretching into 2027 (reported November 2, 2026). That sold-out condition is the load-bearing fact: when the world's leading foundry cannot supply enough leading-edge chips, the theme's necessity is demonstrated by scarcity itself.

## Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo

- **Rare-earth (REMX): the memo's "unclear" is too soft on need.** Weak rare-earth pricing and lithium oversupply are price facts, not need facts: the IEA sees magnet rare-earth demand up 30% by 2030, and the DFARS ban on Chinese NdFeB magnets in US defence contracts takes effect January 1, 2027, so the world needs non-Chinese rare earths structurally even while prices are soft. Necessity 9, not a coin-flip.
- **Lithium (LIT): the memo's "break?" confuses a cyclical oversupply with a need break.** IEA lithium demand more than doubles from 205 kt (2024) to 455 kt (2030) under stated policies; oversupply depresses price, it does not repeal the need for batteries. Necessity 7.
- **Solar (TAN) and clean (PBW, ICLN): the memo overweights US policy.** US tax-credit changes and the December 4 Section 232 tariffs are real headwinds, but the IEA's *Renewables 2026* (November 6, 2026) still sees 3,605 GW of new solar in 2026-2031, three-quarters of all new renewables; the need is global, the policy hit is American. Necessity 7 for TAN, 6 for PBW/ICLN (the -1 is the US subsidy dependence).
- **Silver (SLV, SIL): the memo's "rotation" understates a real demand impairment.** The Silver Institute's November 12, 2026 update has total silver demand down 5% in 2026 with PV silver down ~19% on thrifting and substitution, so silver's structural-growth claim is weaker than the memo implies. Necessity 6, not 7.
- **Gold (GLD, GDXJ, GDX): the memo's "rotation" understates a structural buyer.** Central banks bought 321t in Q3 2026, up 42% year-on-year (World Gold Council, November 3, 2026), running at roughly 1,000t a year for four straight years; that lifts gold above a purely cyclical 5. Necessity 6.
- **Crypto-spot (ETHA, IBIT) and crypto-equity (WGMI, BLOK): the memo's "rotation, no thesis break" is the wrong frame for necessity.** Rotation vs break is a cause question; on necessity, spot crypto has no structural need claim at all — it is a discretionary store-of-value bet whose demand rests on adoption and regulatory conditions that may not hold. Necessity 3 regardless of whether the drop is rotation.
- **Oil/gas (OIH, AMLP, XOP, FCG): the memo's "rotation" is fine on cause but necessity is capped.** The IEA sees oil demand plateauing at ~105.5 mb/d by 2030 with combustible-fuel demand peaking as early as 2027 and EVs displacing 5.4 mb/d by 2030; the world still needs oil for petrochemicals and aviation, but there is no structural growth claim. Necessity 6, not higher.
- **Argentina (ARGT): no disagreement on the memo's "unclear", but the necessity read is harsher.** The memo treats Milei risk as a two-way overhang; on necessity the theme is a discretionary single-country bet whose demand rests on reforms surviving the 2027 election. Necessity 3.
