Top-3 notes: SMH — The global semiconductor market is forecast to reach $1.51 trillion in 2026, up 90% year over year, with a further 27% rise to ~$1.9 trillion in 2027 (WSTS Spring 2026 forecast, released Nov 20, 2026). That makes silicon the one input no AI, defence, auto or consumer system can route around in 5–10 years, which is why semis sit at the top of this ballot despite the AI-capex scare. GRID — About 75% of anticipated US power-demand growth over the next decade is expected to come from data centres, requiring the equivalent of almost 30 GW of power per year, and McKinsey finds a near-term power shortfall more likely than an overbuild (McKinsey, 2026). Transmission and distribution spending of ~$600 billion is projected through 2030 (J.P. Morgan Private Bank, 2026), so the grid buildout is a physical necessity, not a preference. SOXX — AI semiconductors are expected to be ~30% of total semiconductor revenue in 2026, with hyperscaler AI-infrastructure spending up more than 50% in the year (Gartner, Apr 8, 2026). Memory is the sharpest edge of that need: DRAM revenue alone is projected to nearly triple in 2026 to $418.6 billion on HBM and DDR demand from hyperscalers (IDC, Apr 2026).

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- The memo's crypto-spot note ("no change to the scarcity thesis") is a cause observation, not a necessity one: scarcity does not establish need, so IBIT/ETHA score 4 on adoption-conditional demand, not higher.
- The memo's "break" labels on china and argentina are cause calls; on necessity alone China still scores 5 (real, cyclical demand in a huge economy) and Argentina 3 (demand rests on reform conditions that may not hold) — no conflict, but the labels should not be read as necessity verdicts.
- Otherwise none: the memo's nuclear (uranium near multi-year highs), gold (central-bank buying steady), silver (demand falling yet deficit persisting), lithium (oversupply) and clean/solar (policy-dependent) characterisations all support the bands used above.
