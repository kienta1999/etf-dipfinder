Top-3 notes:
- **XLU (10):** The IEA's *Electricity 2026* report (published Feb 2026) puts global electricity demand growth at 3.6% per year through 2030 — "the fastest pace in 15 years" — with US demand rising ~2%/yr and data centers driving roughly half of that increase (ecofinagency summary). Electricity demand is the definition of essential: it doesn't get deferred in a downturn, it just gets repriced, which is why utilities are the necessity ceiling everything else is measured against.
- **IGF (9):** The same IEA report identifies the grid as the binding constraint: more than 2,500 GW of generation, storage, and large-load projects — including data centers — are currently stalled in connection queues worldwide, and grid investment (~$550B/yr per BloombergNEF) must rise about 50% to keep pace. The world doesn't just need the electrons; it physically cannot get them without this build-out, making grid/infra demand as close to mandatory as a cyclical equity theme gets.
- **GRID (9):** The purest expression of that bottleneck: the IEA's *World Energy Investment 2026* (May 2026) shows grid spending up nearly 20% year-on-year to ~$550B, part of the $2.2T flowing into clean energy versus $1.2T for fossil fuels (ethicalmarketingnews). GRID holds the equipment/engineering suppliers directly selling into that gap, so its demand maps 1:1 onto the IEA's "must build" constraint — with record fund inflows ($283.7M in 5 days per the dossier) showing buyers treat it as essential exposure, not a trade.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- **Housing: dossier's quick-pass labels ITB/XHB "break — demand destruction."** On the necessity lens that inverts the finding: demand itself is essential, not destroyed — the US entered 2026 short 4.03M homes (Realtor.com Housing Supply Gap Report, March 2026; Zillow puts it at 4.7M, July 2026). What the dossier calls demand destruction is an *access/affordability* problem (30y mortgage at 7.12%), not an absence of need; the shortage is a decade of underbuilding deep. Necessity scores both 8. (Lens difference, not a factual dispute — the cause lens was right that the *price path* is broken.)
- **Lithium: cause vetoed LIT at 3 on resurgent oversupply; necessity scores it 7.** This is the split the brief anticipated: oversupply is a *price* problem, not a demand problem — global lithium demand is still expected to grow 14–30% in 2026 (Reuters calculation off UBS data, via sweetcrudereports), with energy-storage lithium demand up 55% and on track for ~31% of total consumption. Every EV and every grid battery still needs the metal; the world needs lithium at $12k/t just as much as at $80k/t.
- **China (KWEB): dossier's quick-pass says "break... not a sale."** Necessity 6 disagrees on the framing: the underlying demand is intact and large — 23.8T yuan (~$3.39T) in digital consumption in 2025, the world's largest online retail market for the 13th consecutive year (Xinhua, 2026-01-16), and +8% yoy online retail growth in Q1 2026 (Ministry of Commerce). The "break" is in investability/policy risk (cause's call), not in whether a billion-plus consumers still need the internet.
- **Clean (ICLN/PBW/QCLN): dossier says "rotation" with "no dated catalyst."** Necessity adds a harder number the dossier understates: $2.2T of the $3.4T in 2026 global energy investment goes to clean energy, nearly double fossil fuels' $1.2T (IEA World Energy Investment 2026, May 2026) — and solar alone draws $365B, ~$1B/day. The demand isn't just rotating through rates; it's the single largest capital allocation in the global energy system. PBW stays one notch lower (7) because its small-cap basket is the least demand-certain way to own that flow.
