Top-3 notes:
- GRID (10): The load-bearing fact is the IEA Electricity 2026 finding that global power demand grows ~3.6%/yr to 2030 — 2.5x faster than overall energy demand — while ~2,500 GW of projects sit stuck in grid connection queues. There is no substitute for the wires themselves, so grid equipment scores the ceiling.
- XLU (10): Utilities deliver the electricity every other theme consumes, and the IEA expects data centres to drive roughly half of advanced-economy demand growth through 2030 after a 17% jump in data-centre power use in 2025. Shelter-from-rates is a price question, not a necessity question — the world cannot route around power delivery.
- REMX (9): IEA-derived data show clean-tech demand for magnet rare earths doubling from 19kt in 2024 to 38kt in 2030, with China holding ~94% of sintered NdFeB magnet production — high-performance magnets have no performance-equal substitute in defence and EV motors. It stops one point short of 10 only because recycling could cut primary demand (IEA: up to 35% by 2050) and motor makers are thrifting heavy rare earths.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- The dossier groups all six ai/robot funds as one rotation theme; on necessity they split sharply — AIPO (AI power infrastructure, 9) and QTUM (quantum, 6) are not the same need, and treating the theme as homogeneous would misstate both.
- The dossier's silver "rotation" framing understates the substitution evidence: the Silver Institute (Apr 2026) explicitly forecasts falling PV silver demand from thrifting and outright substitution, which caps SLV at Band 7 rather than a critical-materials score.
- Otherwise none — the dossier's cause buckets are a different lens and are not contradicted on necessity grounds.
