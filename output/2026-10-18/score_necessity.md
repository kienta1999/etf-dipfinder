Top-3 notes:
- XLU: The load-bearing fact is EIA's record-demand path, US power use rising from a record 4,195bn kWh in 2025 to 4,288bn in 2026 and 4,356bn in 2027 on data centres and electrification (EIA STEO via Reuters, 2026-10-06). Electricity has no substitute at system scale, so a regulated utility basket scores the full 10-band rather than a growth discount.
- GRID: The load-bearing fact is the grid bottleneck itself, with IEA putting 2,500 GW of generation, storage and large-load projects stalled in connection queues and calling for annual grid investment to rise 50 percent by 2030 (IEA Electricity 2026, 2026-02-06). Generation can be substituted technology for technology, but every route still needs the network GRID owns.
- PAVE: The load-bearing fact is ASCE's USD 3.7tn gap between planned US infrastructure spending and a state of good repair, up from USD 2.59tn four years earlier (ASCE 2025 Report Card, 2025-03-25). It loses one point from the 10-band only because its roads-and-water breadth is more cyclical construction exposure than pure electricity networks.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- TAN is called a provisional break, but on necessity that break is a US residential-credit story, not a theme story: globally solar is about 80 percent of IEA's 4,600 GW of renewable growth to 2030, so TAN scores 6, not a collapse score.
- Crypto-equity is called rotation with demand intact, but necessity disagrees on the theme itself: even a clean rotation leaves blockchain and especially mining discretionary and substitutable, hence 3 for BLOK/BKCH and 2 for WGMI.
- QTUM is grouped with AI/robot as one rotation note, but necessity splits it sharply: broad AI and robotics score 8 on labour shortage and hyperscaler capex, while quantum scores 4 because Gartner expects over half its startups to fail by 2030 for lack of viable commercial revenue.
- DTCR and VNQ are grouped as pure rate proxies, but necessity splits them: data-centre space scores 7 on JLL's 62 GW of leased additions to 2030, while broad REITs score 5 as cyclical property with no structural growth claim.
- Housing is called rotation on rates alone, which understates necessity: a 4.03m-home US supply gap makes builders a 9, one point below civilisation-critical, not a mid-table cyclical.
