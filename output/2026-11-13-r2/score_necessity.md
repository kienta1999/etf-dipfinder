Top-3 notes:
- **SMH (semis, 9):** WSTS's Spring 2026 release (2026-06-02, PDF fetched) projects the chip market at $1.51T in 2026 (+90%) and ~$1.9T in 2027 (+27%), with every region growing. Nothing in AI, policy or substitute tech routes around semiconductors; the one point off 10 is that the 2026 surge is ~250% memory-price growth, which is cyclical.
- **SOXX (semis, 9):** It is the same theme fact. The memory split in WSTS (memory +32% vs logic +27% in 2027) is also why the memory-only DRAM gets 8, not 9. Necessity holds through the Nov 5 selloff, since that was a price/capex-sentiment event, not a demand forecast change (WSTS 2026-06-02; the Q2 update of Aug 2026 recalculates 2027 at ~$2.1T).
- **GRID (grid/infra, 9):** The IEA's "Building the Future Transmission Grid" (Feb 2025, PDF fetched and read) finds lead times of up to four years for large power transformers and over five years for DC cables, nearly doubled since 2021, with transformer prices up ~75%. That is physical scarcity of a no-substitute input, and electrification plus data-centre load (IEA ~945 TWh by 2030) keeps demand rising.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- **Nuclear:** the memo frames the drop as "partly AI-power unwind". On necessity the AI leg is not load-bearing: WNA (2025-11-13) projects 1,428 GWe by 2050 on national targets alone, so nuclear scores 8 whatever happens to data-centre sentiment.
- **Silver:** the memo calls it pure rate rotation. On necessity the structural industrial story has weakened: the Silver Institute (2026-04-15) sees PV silver demand -19% in 2026 from thrifting and substitution, so silver sits at 6, below the grid metals.
- **Lithium:** I agree the selloff is beta, but sodium-ion is now a commercial competing chemistry (CATL mass production in 2026; CRU ~10% share by 2030), which caps lithium at band 7 despite the IEA's >3x demand to 2040.
- **Crypto-equity:** the memo notes miners "now trade as AI-infra proxies". That AI pivot is a different theme borrowed by the miners; the mining theme itself (record-low hashprice in 2026) is discretionary, hence 4.
- **Grid/infra:** the memo flags the Texas data-centre moratorium as a thesis dent. For necessity it moves where load gets built, not whether grid capacity is needed. Transformer scarcity (IEA, Feb 2025) says the grid is under-built, so GRID stays 9. PAVE is 7, not 9, because its demand leans on federal highway money (the Dec 11 IIJA lapse).
- **Rare-earth:** the memo leans on policy (DFARS, the KL truce). Those are catalysts, not necessity. The Adamas shortage forecast (206kt NdFeB by 2035) is from April 2022 and too stale to carry an 8, and the IEA 2026 sees only ~+50% rare-earth demand to 2040, so REMX holds 7.
