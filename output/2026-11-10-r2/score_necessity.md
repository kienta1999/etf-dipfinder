## Top-3 notes

**GRID (9).** The IEA's 2026 transmission-grid report counts 1,650 GW of solar and wind projects in advanced development awaiting grid connections in 2024, and says transmission spending must exceed $200bn a year by the mid-2030s from $140bn in 2023. Every new generator, EV and data centre needs wires, and no technology routes around the grid itself — the constraint is build pace, not demand.

**PSI (9).** McKinsey's September 2026 semiconductor revision puts the five big hyperscalers at about $800bn of capex in 2026 and $1tn in 2027, almost all of it AI infrastructure, lifting its 2030 chip-market base case to $2.3tn at a 19% CAGR. Server and data-centre chip revenue alone is projected to rise from $330bn in 2025 to $1.2tn in 2030, which is why the theme sits at the top of the necessity ranking despite its cyclicality.

**SOXX (9).** SEMI said on September 1, 2026 that the global semiconductor market will exceed $1.5tn in 2026 and cross $2tn by 2030, driven by AI data centres demanding advanced logic, high-bandwidth memory and enterprise SSDs. The growth has also spread beyond AI chips into equipment, testing and advanced packaging, so a broad semis basket captures a structurally rising need rather than a single product cycle.

## Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo

- **Silver — the memo overstates demand intactness.** It calls the crash rotation with no demand collapse, but the Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 (April 2026) shows solar silver use fell 6% in 2025 and is forecast to fall a further 19% in 2026 to ~151 Moz as makers thrift silver per cell — the theme's largest growth market is structurally using less, so silver is capped at 6, not 7.
- **Gold — reserve buying is real but total demand is flat.** The memo correctly cites Q3 central-bank buying of 321t (+42% y/y), confirmed by the World Gold Council on November 3, 2026, but total Q3 demand was 1% lower y/y with bar-and-coin demand down 19% and jewellery at its weakest Q3 since 2000 — a store-of-value bid, not a growth theme, so gold stays at 6.
- **Crypto-equity — the memo's "no thesis break" is about BTC, not the equities.** Spot Bitcoin ETFs hold over $128bn and 172 public companies hold BTC, but miners and exchanges are 3–4x beta claims whose demand rests entirely on BTC's price and continued adoption; the world does not need crypto miners in 5–10 years, so the theme is 5.
- **Lithium — agree with the memo's oversupply warning, disagree that it dents necessity.** The IEA's Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 still has lithium as the fastest-growing critical mineral, with demand more than tripling by 2040 on EVs and storage; oversupply is a price cycle, not a demand break, so LIT stays at 7.
- **Argentina — the memo's "cheap market nobody wants" understates the conditionality.** Growth is real (IMF ~4% in 2026 per the April WEO, BNP Paribas February 2026) and the resource endowment is real (Vaca Muerta, lithium/copper), but reserves remain thin against $15bn of dollar debt service in 2026 and $29bn in 2027 — demand for the theme rests on reforms holding, so ARGT is 4.
- **Nuclear — agree with the memo.** The IAEA/OECD NEA Red Book 2026 (September 14, 2026) puts uranium requirements at 84,800–143,900 tU by 2050 versus ~64,500 tU now, confirming the +50–144% demand growth the memo cites; nuclear scores 8.
