Top-3 notes:
- IGF: The IEA (reported by Rigzone, Nov 2025) models US datacenter electricity demand rising from 120.65 TWh in 2021 to well over 400 TWh in 2030, accounting for roughly half of all new US electricity demand growth through the decade. No software or market mechanism routes around this: every AI training run must land on a wire, making grid capacity the physical precondition of the most valuable growth sector in the economy.
- PAVE: The IEA (via pv-tech, Nov 2025) reports that more than 2,500 GW of projects — renewables, storage, and large loads like data centers — are currently stalled in connection queues worldwide, a stockpile that exists precisely because grids cannot expand at the speed of generation. A demand chokepoint measured in terawatts is the load-bearing fact: grid investment is not a growth option, it is the release valve.
- XLU: The US Energy Information Administration (via Reuters, Sept 9, 2026) expects national power consumption to set records in both 2026 and 2027 on datacenter and electrification load. Electricity is the one theme on this ballot for which no substitute, AI workaround, or policy reversal can reduce the underlying physical need — every other theme ultimately draws its necessity from electrons being available.
