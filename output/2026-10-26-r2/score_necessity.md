Top-3 notes:
- GRID (10): The load-bearing fact is IEA Electricity 2026 (2026-02-06): global power demand grows ~3.6%/yr through 2030, at least 2.5x overall energy demand, and annual grid investment must rise ~50% by 2030 from ~$400B. There is no substitute for the grid that carries that demand — every other energy/AI theme on this ballot plugs into it.
- SMH (10): The load-bearing fact is McKinsey's 2026 revision of the 2030 semiconductor market to $2.3T, up 44% from its autumn-2025 $1.6T estimate, driven by AI data-centre chips and HBM. No sector — defence, grids, autos, medicine — has a non-chip route around that substrate in 5-10 years.
- COPX (10): The load-bearing fact is the IEA's critical-minerals outlook: announced copper mine supply falls ~25% short of primary requirements in 2035, against demand the IEA projects up over 25% by 2040 from grids, EVs and data centres. Copper's conductivity role in electrification has no at-scale substitute, which is what puts a miner fund in the civilisation-critical band.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Silver (dossier C: "no demand break identified", pure rotation): on necessity, the Silver Institute's 2026 forecast itself shows industrial demand falling ~3% on PV thrifting/substitution — substitution is a live necessity cap at band 7, not a non-event.
- Crypto-equity (dossier C: "not a crypto demand break" because IBIT is a leader): agreed on cause, opposite on necessity — spot-ETF strength is precisely what routes demand around mining/blockchain equities, so proxy necessity is band 3, not Bitcoin's.
- China (dossier C: fundamental drag "unproven either way"): on necessity the structural evidence is in — a 4.5-5% 2026 GDP target, the lowest since 1991 (UBS, 2026-02-06), and an IMF path fading to ~3.5% by 2030 put this in band 5, not band 7.
- Clean/solar (dossier C: "unclear", citing Sunrun's -31% subscriber adds): that is a US residential cause signal, not a necessity break — IEA Renewables 2025 still has global renewable capacity doubling by 2030 with solar ~80% of additions, so necessity stays at band 7.
- Nuclear (dossier C: rotation, record $96.50/lb uranium price): agreed the demand side is intact, but necessity is capped at 8, not 10 — the Energy Transitions Commission (2026-10-06) notes solar/wind are 20-50% cheaper than new nuclear in most developed countries, a visible competing technology under the band-7 anchor.
