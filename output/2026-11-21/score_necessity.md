Top-3 notes:
- GRID: The load-bearing fact is the IEA's Electricity 2026 finding that annual grid investment must rise 50% by 2030 from ~$400B, with over 2,500 GW of renewables, storage and data-centre projects stalled in connection queues worldwide. No generation technology, however cheap, delivers power without wires, so grid is the one theme in this panel with no substitute and a quantified investment gap.
- PSI: The load-bearing fact is McKinsey's 2026 revision lifting the semiconductor market to $2.3T by 2030, 44% above its autumn-2025 $1.6T estimate, on hyperscaler capex of ~$800B in 2026 and ~$1T in 2027. That is a structural demand step-change, not a cycle, because AI data-centre buildout is the driver and leading-edge supply is capacity-constrained.
- XSD: The load-bearing fact is TSMC's May 2026 guidance that the global chip market will exceed $1.5T by 2030 with AI/HPC at 55% of it, and that AI accelerator wafer demand is projected to rise 11-fold from 2022 to 2026. XSD shares PSI's necessity case; its small/mid-cap tilt does not change the theme-level need for chips.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Lithium "BREAK": the memo's break is a price/oversupply story (SQM warning, current glut), not a necessity story — the IEA still projects lithium demand more than tripling by 2040 and EV battery deployment reaching ~3 TWh by 2030, so necessity stays 7 even if the cycle is weak.
- Silver "ROTATION": the memo treats silver as a rates/dollar rotation, but the Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 shows the structural problem is substitution — solar silver use fell 6% in 2025 and is forecast -19% in 2026 despite record panel builds — which caps necessity at 6, not a rotation-only 7.
- Clean/solar "UNCLEAR leaning break": the memo leans on US policy headwinds, but globally solar is ~80% of 2025-30 renewables growth (3.6 TW) and clean investment is $2.2T in 2026, ~2x fossil fuels per the IEA — policy slows the US leg, it does not erase the 5-10y need, so clean 7 / solar 8.
- Crypto-spot "BREAK leaning": the memo frames the drop as a flow story with "no thesis change in the asset itself", but on necessity the thesis was never a world need — BTC/ETH demand rests on institutional belief holding, which is a band-3 condition, so IBIT 4 / ETHA 3 rather than a rotation-grade score.
- Grid/infra "ROTATION": the memo correctly calls the selloff rotation, but understates the necessity side — a 50% rise in grid investment by 2030 and 2,500 GW queued is a civilisation-critical bottleneck, which is why GRID scores 10, the only 10 in this ballot.
- Argentina "UNCLEAR": the memo notes the Milei reform story is "intact but fragile" — on necessity that fragility is the point: a single-country theme whose demand rests on one election (Oct 2027) and IMF support is band 3, not an unclear 5.
