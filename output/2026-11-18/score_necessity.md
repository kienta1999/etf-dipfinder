Top-3 notes: GRID (10): the IEA's Electricity 2026 report (Feb 2026) says global electricity demand grows >3.5% a year through 2030 and grid investment must rise 50% by 2030, with 2,500 GW of projects stuck in connection queues — the grid, not generation, is the bottleneck. There is no substitute for wires and transformers to move that power, which is why this is the only 10 on the ballot.
REMX (9): the IEA's 2026 data put China at 91% of rare-earth refining and 94% of sintered magnet production, and NdFeB magnets have no performance substitute in EV motors, wind turbines and defence systems (a single F-35 needs ~417kg of rare earths). The one-point deduction from 10 is for emerging substitutes — iron-nitride magnets and ferrites — which are real but not yet at scale.
SMH (9): McKinsey's Oct 2026 revision puts the semiconductor market at $2.3tn by 2030 (19% CAGR, up from 13%), with hyperscalers guiding $720–740bn of 2026 capex, and SK Hynix's chief says the wafer shortage runs to 2030. The one-point deduction from 10 is cyclicality: this demand is concentrated in one AI buildout, and a capex pause would cut it sharply.

- Nuclear: agree with the memo that the long-term demand case strengthened (IAEA demand +50–144% by 2050, existing-mine output halving by 2040) — scored 8, the top of the substitutable-growth band, not 10, because gas and renewables+storage compete for the same firm-power job.
