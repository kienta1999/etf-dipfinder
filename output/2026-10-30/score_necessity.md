Top-3 notes:
GRID: The IEA's Electricity 2026 report (Feb 2026) says annual grid investment must rise about 50% by 2030 to roughly $600 billion, because more than 2,500 GW of generation, storage and large-load projects are stalled in connection queues. That queue is the load-bearing fact: without wires, none of the solar, nuclear or data-centre themes in this ballot can deliver power.
REMX: The IEA's 2026 rare-earth report says magnet demand has doubled since 2015 and rises a further 30% by 2030 under current policies, with permanent magnets covering about 95% of rare-earth consumption by value. That value concentration is the load-bearing fact: the theme's necessity is a magnet story, not a broad basket of minor metals.
PAVE: The same IEA Electricity 2026 finding — grid investment up ~50% to ~$600 billion a year by 2030 — underpins PAVE's grid sleeve, while US reshoring and infrastructure spend supports its broader holdings. The one-point deduction from band 10 reflects that roads, airports and water systems in the basket are deferrable in a way transmission wires are not.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Silver: the memo says "no demand collapse in the metal itself" — true for total demand, but it understates thrifting: the Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 forecasts PV silver down ~19% in 2026 (186.6 to ~151 Moz), a per-unit demand erosion that caps silver at band 7 rather than higher.
- Nuclear: the memo's rotation framing is consistent with my scores, but I do not lift nuclear to band 10 — uranium demand growth (WNA: ~69 ktU in 2025 to >150 ktU in 2040) does not make nuclear electricity unsubstitutable, since solar, wind and gas can supply the same electrons.
- Gold: the memo cites record ETF holdings and continued central-bank buying, which I accept (863t in 2025, ~850t projected for 2026), but that is monetary, not civilisation-critical, demand — hence 6, not 7 or higher.
- Crypto-equity: the memo's "rotation leaning break" understates the necessity weakness — WGMI/BKCH score 3 because miner necessity is entirely derivative of bitcoin adoption, a condition that may not hold, rather than a standalone world need.
- Clean/solar: the memo's "thesis intact" holds, but the IEA's Renewables 2025 cut to the US forecast (nearly 50% lower on tax-credit phase-out) is a real policy-substitution risk that keeps TAN/ICLN/QCLN/PBW at band 7, not above it.
