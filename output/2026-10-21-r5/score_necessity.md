Top-3 notes:
- XLU (9): NERC's 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment (January 2026, primary PDF) projects US summer peak demand up more than 224 GW over 10 years, 69% above the prior year's 132 GW, and winter up 245 GW, mostly from AI data centres. The grid has no substitute at scale; the only route-around is hyperscaler onsite generation, which is why it sits at 9, not 10.
- COPX (9): S&P Global's 2026-01-08 study (primary release fetched) projects copper demand of 42 Mt by 2040, 50% above today, against mine output peaking at 33 Mt in 2030. That leaves a 10 Mt (25%) shortfall even after scrap more than doubles to 10 Mt, with AI/data centres and defense each roughly tripling.
- NLR (8): WNA's World Nuclear Fuel Report 2025 (September 2025) has Reference reactor requirements rising from 68,920 tU (2025) to over 150,000 tU in 2040, and warns of mine-supply gaps after 2030. Nuclear stays in the substitutable band (+1) because gas and renewables plus storage compete for the same firm-power demand; NLR's utility sleeve also gets the NERC load-growth tailwind.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Silver: the memo says "bubble deflation vs rotation not separable." On the demand side, the Silver Institute (April 2026) projects PV silver demand down 19% and total industrial demand down 3% in 2026 from thrifting and copper substitution. So the industrial scarcity story behind the 2025-26 rally has weakened, not just been rotated out of.
- Solar: the memo lists "no October story; policy-driven". There is a structural fact it misses: BNEF expects 2026 to be the first annual drop in global solar installs in about two decades (China market-pricing reform). That is why TAN scores below ICLN on this lens.
- Defense-EU: the memo calls peace-talk headlines "the 2025 driver". On necessity the anchor is NATO's codified 3.5% core + 1.5% by 2035, which holds regardless of a Ukraine settlement. European allies (core average 2.86% in 2026) have the largest gap to close.
- The memo treats the dips as "one rotation out of the real assets / scarcity trade". On this lens the scarcity claims are not equal: copper (10 Mt 2040 gap), grid power and uranium have measured structural shortfalls, while silver (thrifting), lithium (sodium-ion) and rare-earth (export-control suspension, rare-earth-free motors) each face a visible substitute.
