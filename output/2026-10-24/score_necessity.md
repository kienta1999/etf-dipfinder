Top-3 notes:
- GRID (grid/infra): The load-bearing fact is the IEA's grid arithmetic — investment must nearly double to over $600 billion per year by 2030 and 80 million km of lines must be added or refurbished by 2040, roughly the entire existing global grid (IEA, Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions). No technology routes around the wires themselves: every data centre, EV, solar farm and nuclear plant in the other high-necessity themes needs this one first.
- URNM (nuclear): The load-bearing fact is the World Nuclear Association's 5 September 2025 Nuclear Fuel Report — reactor uranium requirements rise 28% by 2030 to about 87,000 tU and above 150,000 tU by 2040, while output from existing mines roughly halves after 2030. That is committed reactor-fleet arithmetic across multiple sovereign states, not a sentiment forecast, which is why nuclear sits in the band-10 family despite partial substitutes for electricity.
- SOXX (semis): The load-bearing fact is McKinsey's September 2026 revision — the semiconductor market forecast for 2030 was raised 44% to $2.3 trillion, with data-centre chip revenue rising from $330 billion in 2025 to $1.2 trillion in 2030 on hyperscaler capex of about $800 billion in 2026. There is no non-semiconductor route to AI, modern vehicles, defence systems or industrial control, so the only deduction from 10 is substitution within the chip stack itself.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Clean is marked "unclear" with subsidy durability as the open question: on necessity that is too cautious — IEA World Energy Investment 2025 puts clean investment at $2.2T in 2025, twice fossil fuels, driven by cost competitiveness and energy security as well as policy, so the theme is band 7 structural, not subsidy-or-nothing.
- Silver is framed on its sixth straight physical deficit: I score it band 7, not higher, because the Silver Institute's own 2026 survey shows PV thrifting and substitution cutting photovoltaic silver demand (186.6 Moz in 2025, forecast down ~19% in 2026) — the deficit coexists with a visible route-around.
- Space is marked "no demand collapse cited": agreed on cause, but necessity is still only 6 — a ~$626B space economy growing toward $1T does not make pre-profit launch and discretionary holdings civilisation-critical, and fibre substitutes for satellite broadband.
- Gold is framed as rotation with central-bank buying intact: agreed, but necessity is 6, not a high score — 863 tonnes of 2025 central-bank buying (WGC) supports a reserve-asset bid, yet gold has no critical industrial use and reserve demand can route to other assets.
- Crypto-equity divergence (spot IBIT leading, miners collapsing) is, on this lens, a necessity signal and not just miner economics: if demand can be fully served by spot ETFs, listed miners and blockchain equities are substitutable by construction, hence band 3 and WGMI at 2.
