Top-3 notes: SMH (semis, 10): McKinsey raised its 2030 semiconductor market forecast to $2.3 trillion, a 19% CAGR through 2030 versus a 9% historical average, with server/data-centre chip revenue rising from $330B in 2025 to $1.2T by 2030. That is the single load-bearing fact because it makes chips a structural, no-substitute input to the AI buildout rather than a cyclical trade. URNM (nuclear, 9): the IAEA's September 2026 projections put global nuclear capacity at 377 GW at end-2025 rising to 1,045 GW by 2050 in its high case, with US utilities facing ~186Mlb of unfilled uranium requirements for 2026-2035. That is the load-bearing fact because uranium demand is contractually locked in by reactors that take 5-10 years to build while mines take 10-15 years. GRID (grid/infra, 9): the IEA's Electricity 2026 report counts 2,500+ GW of projects stalled in grid-connection queues and says annual grid investment must rise ~50% from ~$400B to ~$600B by 2030. That is the load-bearing fact because the grid, not generation, is now the binding constraint on electrification and AI power.
Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Clean (PBW/ICLN): the memo stresses tax credits are gone, but I do not score clean at 1 (subsidy-dependent with subsidy gone) — Lazard's July 2026 LCOE+ finds unsubsidised renewables still the cheapest new-build generation, so the need survives the subsidy; score 6.

- Gold (GLD/GDX/GDXJ): the memo says demand is intact via central-bank buying, and I agree but only to 6 — 321t in Q3 (+42% y/y) lifts gold one point above the cyclical band 5, not to 7, since gold has no structural growth claim.
- Argentina/Brazil (ARGT/EWZ): the memo's "break" is a cause call on the Lula runoff shock; on necessity alone both are band 3 single-country discretionary exposures, which is consistent with, not a contradiction of, the memo.
- Nuclear (URNM/URA/NLR): no disagreement — the memo's "rotation, thesis intact" plus the IAEA capacity path supports a 9, one point below the civilisation-critical band for substitutable baseload alternatives.
