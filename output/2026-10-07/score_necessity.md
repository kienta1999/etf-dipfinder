Top-3 notes:

XLU (10): The EIA's October 6, 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook projected US power consumption at a record 4,288 billion kWh in 2026 rising to 4,356 billion kWh in 2027, driven by AI data centers and electrification — and Texas demand alone is expected to grow 10-15% annually through 2027 (source: Reuters/EIA, 2026-10-06). This is load growth on the grid's physical infrastructure, not financial sentiment: it cannot be routed around, only served.

IGF (9): The IEA's World Energy Investment 2026 (May 2026) forecast global electricity grid spending at ~$550 billion in 2026, up nearly 20% year-on-year, with total electricity supply and infrastructure spending at ~$1.6 trillion — electricity is now the dominant driver of all global energy investment. Every electron from the AI-driven demand surge above has to travel through wires someone must build, making grid investment as close to non-substitutable as infrastructure gets.

Nuclear theme — URNM/NLR/URA (9): The IEA reports ~80 GW of new nuclear capacity under construction across 15 countries with nuclear investment exceeding $80B annually, and 39 countries have committed to tripling capacity by 2050; the World Nuclear Association puts 2025 uranium demand at ~68,920 tonnes rising to ~107,000 tonnes by 2040 under its reference scenario (IEA WEI 2026 / WNA via carboncredits, 2026). No other carbon-free source offers 24/7 baseload at data-center scale, so the fuel demand is structural — the one-point deduction is that wind/solar plus storage remain credible competitors for incremental electricity share.
