Top-3 notes:
- SMH (semis): The load-bearing fact is that semiconductors have no functional substitute and the market is still expanding structurally, with PwC putting it above $1 trillion by 2030 from about $627 billion in 2024 on AI, auto and data-centre demand (PwC Semiconductor Outlook, reported 2025). That makes this a band-10 theme rather than a cyclical tech trade, even though individual chip cycles remain volatile.
- GRID (grid/infra): The load-bearing fact is IEA Electricity 2026 forecasting global electricity demand growth averaging 3.6% a year over 2026-2030, 50% faster than the prior decade, with grid investment needing to rise about 50% by 2030 (IEA, iea.org, fetched this run). Electricity networks cannot be routed around by software, so demand for grid equipment is civilisation-critical.
- COPX (base-metals / copper): The load-bearing fact is the IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 still projecting a copper supply deficit in 2035, narrowed only from about 30% to 25% as new projects advance (IEA, iea.org, fetched this run). Copper's conductivity role across grids, EVs and data centres has no at-scale substitute, which pins the copper miners at band 10.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- The dossier groups QTUM inside ai/robot as one unclear theme; on necessity it splits sharply — quantum scores 4 (Gartner, 2026-10-08) while the AI and robotics funds score 7-8.
- The dossier groups DTCR with VNQ as one unclear reit theme; on necessity they split 8 versus 5, because data-centre scarcity (CBRE vacancy 1.4% in 2025) is structural and broad REIT demand is only cyclical.
- The dossier calls silver and gold both rotation; on necessity silver (6, industrial 657.4 Moz) sits on a different footing from a purely cyclical metal, and gold (6) is supported by structural central-bank reserve demand (863t in 2025), not by productive use.
- The dossier calls housing pure rotation; on necessity the 4.03 million US home deficit in 2025 (Realtor.com, 2026-03-03) is a structural shelter need, scoring ITB 8 despite a rate-driven cause for the dip.
