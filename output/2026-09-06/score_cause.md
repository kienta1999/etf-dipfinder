Top-3 notes:
- **GLD (9).** Gold *rose* 9.6% in August to $4,429 (Aug 31) and only broke down after Warsh's inflation speech pushed implied September hike odds to ~64-70% and the 10y to ~4.79% (RioTimes, 2026-09-02). Every named driver is a discount-rate driver; nothing in the search touched central-bank buying, supply, or the debasement bid, and GDX/GDXJ leading the metal by ~21-24% over 3m is the shape of a rates dip, not a broken metal.
- **NLR (9).** Uranium futures hit $90/lb in August, the highest in over six months, on supply setbacks and data-center demand, while Cameco fell ~19% and NexGen ~16% over the same month (TradingEconomics, Aug 2026; ts2.tech). When the commodity makes a 6-month high and the equities make a 6-month low, the seller is a position, not a thesis.
- **URA (9).** Same fact set, and utilities remain structurally under-contracted into the 2030s (PRNewswire, 2026-08), so the demand curve behind the theme did not move at all this quarter. The only cause-relevant risk is that this is the most crowded AI-adjacent trade on the board and unwinds can run long.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- **The macro premise is stale/overstated.** The memo's "10y 4.82%, ~66% odds of a September *hike*" was the late-August peak. By 2026-09-04 the 10y had pulled back off three-year highs to ~4.68-4.76% and hike odds slid to ~50%, with Goldman calling a September hike "very unlikely" on softer retail sales, a slowing labour market and cooling inflation. A *fading* rate scare makes GLD/SLV/XLU/ITB more clearly rotation, not less — I score them higher than the memo's caveat implies, and I do not accept "this whole list gets cheaper if they hike" as the base case.
- **UFO is not a permanent re-rating of the theme.** UFO added SpaceX at a 6.17% weight on 2026-06-16 as its top holding, with index rules allowing a scale-up to 15% (VettaFi reconstitution). The fund now owns the thing that took its premium. Still only a 4 — the other ~94% was priced on SpaceX scarcity and genuinely re-rated — but "that multiple doesn't come back" is wrong as written.
- **TAN is not a clean "break".** OBBBA passed in 2025 and was fully known when TAN ran +120% in five months into June 2026 on data-center demand. The current drawdown is give-back plus new component tariffs plus the residential earnings reset, so it is mixed cause (4), not a fresh policy repeal. PBW keeps the low score — worse holdings, and its own drawdown history says nothing unusual is even happening.
- **SHLD is not pure intra-defense rotation.** Reuters and CNBC both frame the European cooling as investors reassessing the war-winner thesis itself: peace-in-Ukraine risk, changed doctrine, fragmented procurement, US pressure to buy American, on valuations richer than tech. Part thesis, part flow → 6, below XAR.
- **KWEB is not "unclear".** The mechanism is identifiable: an investment cycle compressing reported earnings, with roughly half of Alibaba's ¥380bn three-year commitment still unspent. That is bad for the next few prints and reversible, unlike 2021's regulatory action. Cause 6.
