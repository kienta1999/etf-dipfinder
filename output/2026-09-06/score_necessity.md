Top-3 notes
- **XLU (9):** The load-bearing fact is that this is the first structural US electricity demand growth since the 1990s — S&P Global (2025-10-14) has data-center grid demand nearly tripling by 2030 to ~134 GW, and BofA/utility IRPs show demand outpacing planned capacity additions by >100 GW. Every AI outcome, bull or bear, runs through a wire that a regulated monopoly owns; the "AI power premium" can deflate without the need deflating.
- **URA (9):** Uranium is the only theme here with literally zero substitution path — a reactor with a 60-year life is a fuel contract, not a preference — and the WNA 2025 Fuel Report (Sep 2025) has demand +28% by 2030 and >150,000 tU by 2040 against mine output from current deposits halving over 2030-40. 80 reactors are physically under construction today, so the demand is poured in concrete rather than legislated.
- **URNM (9):** Identical necessity to URA on this lens; the AI angle is the differentiator versus 2007's uranium cycle — hyperscalers are signing PPAs for firm clean baseload that only nuclear supplies at scale, so the technology that was supposed to disrupt everything is the one making this one more necessary.

Disagreements with the first-pass memo
- Memo calls SLV's "industrial/monetary thesis untouched." It isn't: the Silver Institute forecasts PV silver demand −19% in 2026 on thrifting. That is a live demonstration that silver's growth leg CAN be engineered around when price rises — the exact failure mode this lens exists to catch. Deficit widens on shrinking mine supply, not on demand.
- Memo frames XLU as "the safe sector is now a risk canary" (AI-power premium deflating). On necessity XLU is the strongest name on the board; a deflating premium is a price fact, not a demand fact.
- Memo treats REMX as two theses pulling opposite ways, with lithium oversupply as the drag. On necessity both legs are needed and NdPr is in deficit *now*; the Western need for non-Chinese magnet supply is price-insensitive and strategic, so I score it well above what "unclear" implies.
- Memo lumps TAN with PBW as "avoid — same OBBBA repeal." On need they are far apart: global solar keeps compounding to 864 GW by 2035 without US credits (TAN 6), while PBW's holdings are the ones whose demand *was* the credit (4).
- Memo says UFO's re-rating is permanent. That is a price/multiple claim; the underlying need compounds ~9%/yr to $1.8tn by 2035, so I do not mark the theme down for it.
- Memo ranks KWEB the #1 candidate. On this lens it is 14th of 15 — the only theme where a plausible technology (AI agents disintermediating discovery and commerce) removes the toll booth entirely.
