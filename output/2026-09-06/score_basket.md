Top-3 notes
- **GLD (9).** The only structures here with zero decay are the two physical trusts: GLD holds allocated bars, reports on a 1099 not a K-1, and its NAV bleeds exactly one thing — the 0.40% fee — with no roll, counterparty or issuer-credit leg. The only honest marks against it are the 28% collectibles rate on gains and the fact that SSGA sells the identical exposure at 0.10% in GLDM, so an investor paying 0.40% is paying for spread and options depth, not for the metal.
- **XLU (9).** At 0.08% it is a fifth to a tenth the fee of every thematic fund on this board, and its constituents are the only cohort here that are uniformly profitable with investment-grade balance sheets — a regulated monopoly's earnings are the highest-quality holdings in the list. The single impurity is that Constellation (~9%) is a merchant/AI-power name rather than a rate-base utility, which means the fund carries a slice of the exact premium that is deflating.
- **SLV (8).** Same trust cleanliness as GLD — physical bars, no futures roll, no K-1 — which matters more in silver than gold because the futures alternative there is genuinely lossy. It scores a notch lower purely on cost mechanics: 0.50% versus 0.40%, on a metal with 42% realised vol and wider physical spreads, so more of the tracking friction is structural.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo
- **KWEB as candidate #1 is the memo's biggest basket blind spot.** Its holdings are the highest-quality equities in the whole list, but the memo never mentions that you do not own them — every position is a Cayman entity with contractual claims on a PRC operating company, a structure KraneShares itself says Chinese law does not guarantee. Great companies, worst legal wrapper on the board: 6, not a top pick.
- **"NLR and URNM are the same trade, more concentrated" is wrong by three points.** NLR's largest position is 8.79% across 28 profitable operators; URNM puts ~48% into three positions and one of them (SPUT, 14.45%) is a closed-end fund inside an ETF — a second fee layer and a NAV discount that can widen independently of uranium. If you want the theme and not the single-name risk, the fund choice is not a detail.
- **URA is not the safe diversified alternative either** — Cameco is 22.06% of it. Every uranium wrapper on this board is a Cameco position with decoration.
- **TAN and PBW are not one "break".** On the fund itself they are 5 and 2: TAN's two largest holdings (First Solar, Nextracker) actually earn money, while PBW is a micro-cap basket that is not even a single theme and mechanically rebalances into its losers each quarter. If the memo is right that the policy is gone, PBW is the one whose holdings need capital markets to survive it.
- **ICLN's "global/utility-scale mix" is scored as a virtue by the memo; on this lens it is impurity.** Bloom Energy at 8.53% is fuel cells and China Yangtze Power at 4.86% is a Chinese hydro utility — roughly half the fund is regulated or non-solar cash flow wearing a clean-energy label. It happens to be the *reason* ICLN holds up, but the buyer should know what they own.
- **UFO's SpaceX problem is forward-looking, not historical.** The memo says SpaceX "took the scarcity premium with it"; the basket fact is that UFO now holds SpaceX at 6.17% with index headroom to 15%, on a shrinking sub-$1bn asset base at 0.75%. The fund is converting from a diversified space basket into a levered SpaceX proxy with 30 satellites attached.
- **SHLD vs XAR is a structure difference the memo treats as pure rotation.** XAR's largest position is 3.8%; SHLD's is Palantir at 10.81%. Whatever happens to European primes, SHLD's tracking error against the defense theme runs through one software multiple.
