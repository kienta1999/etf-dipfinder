## Within-theme divergences

- Nuclear: URNM 9 > NLR 8 = URA 8. URNM is the only basket trapped almost entirely in the fuel chokepoint (miners plus physical uranium). NLR dilutes the chokepoint into regulated nuclear utilities and reactor industrials — themselves necessities, which is why it does not fall further. URA dilutes it into SMR developers and component names.
- Grid/infra: IGF 9 > PAVE 7. IGF owns the operators of existing utilities, airports, toll roads and pipelines; PAVE owns the builders, materials and equipment suppliers whose order books are cyclical capex.
- Defense: SHLD, PPA and XAR 8 > ITA 7. The gap is commercial dilution, not the defense end-use: ITA's GE Aerospace plus Boeing weight makes roughly a third of the fund a commercial-aviation bet.
- Clean/solar: TAN 6 = ICLN 6 > PBW 5. TAN is purer but single-source and subsidy-exposed; ICLN is broader with a large utility/producer weight; PBW spreads into EVs, charging and hydrogen tech where demand is discretionary.
- Housing: ITB 6 > XHB 5. Both sell into the same shelter shortage; ITB is mostly builders, XHB is mostly products and furnishings around the house.
- Space: UFO 5 > ARKX 4. UFO requires space-derived revenue; ARKX admits enablers and conglomerates, so the necessity of satellite services reaches the holder diluted.
- Precious metals: SLV 9 > GLD 7, per the anchor — silver earns its keep industrially (solar, electronics, EVs) on top of the monetary bid; gold's necessity is monetary only. SLV is held one point below the top band because solar manufacturers are actively thrifting silver loadings, a named substitution pressure.

## Disagreements with the dossier quick-pass

- Solar and clean: the quick pass calls TAN a policy break and clean unclear. That is a cause judgment, not necessity — electricity remains must-buy, so TAN, ICLN and PBW stay mid-band (6, 6, 5) rather than falling to discretionary levels; the subsidy dependence is taken as a one-point dock, not a collapse of end-use necessity.
- Space: the quick pass stresses real launch cadence and defense-space demand. On necessity that does not lift UFO or ARKX above 5 and 4 — most commercial space spending is deferrable growth capex, and ARKX as held is not even pure space.
- China: the quick pass notes KWEB fundamentals look the strongest in years. Necessity disagrees on structure, not valuation: an 80%-plus discretionary and media basket scores 4 no matter how cheap or profitable the platforms are.
- Gold vs silver: the quick pass treats both as one hard-asset rotation complex. Necessity splits them by two points, because only silver has a large industrial end-use that must be bought regardless of the monetary cycle.
