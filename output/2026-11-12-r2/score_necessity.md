Top-3 notes:
- **GRID (9).** The IEA's World Energy Investment 2026 (June 2026) puts global grid investment at ~$550B in 2026, up ~20% y/y after years of lagging generation, and nothing routes around physical transmission. The IEA's Energy and AI report (April 2025) adds that data-centre load alone roughly doubles, from 415 TWh in 2024 to ~945 TWh by 2030.
- **COPX (9).** The IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (July 2026) projects copper supply below demand through 2035, with the deficit equal to ~25% of demand. That is a structural shortage in the one metal every electrification theme needs. Aluminium substitution in some cabling keeps it at 9, not 10.
- **SMH, and the semis cluster (9).** WSTS's Spring 2026 forecast (2026-06-02) has the chip market at $1.51T in 2026 (+~90%) and ~$1.9T in 2027, and its Q2 update said actuals ran slightly ahead. Chips are the input to AI, grid, autos and defense alike, with no substitute. The −1 is for cyclicality, not for any substitution risk.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Silver: the memo says "industrial demand is unchanged". The Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 (2026-04-15) shows industrial offtake −3% in 2025, forecast −3% again in 2026, and PV −19% on thrifting and substitution. Industrial demand is shrinking, which is why silver scores band 5, not band 7.
- Lithium: the memo frames it as a 2027 surplus fear. On necessity, Benchmark (2026-10-22) confirms the demand-growth slowdown (storage lithium ~5%/yr to 2030, after >60%/yr), and sodium-ion is a visible substitute. That supports band 7 with no +1.
- Defense-us: the memo's budget-cut risk is a cause and policy question. On necessity, global demand is at a record (SIPRI 2025: $2.887T, 11th straight rise), but the US itself fell 7.5% in 2025, so it stays at band 7 +1, not band 10.
- Horizon note: lenses.md defines necessity over 5-10 years and the task prompt said 3-5 years. The scores above hold on both horizons. No score would change by switching, except that lithium's and solar's near-term slowdowns weigh more on the shorter one, and neither would fall below 7.
