## Top-3 notes

- **GRID (9).** The IEA's grid report (via AP/PBS, 2023-10-17) says the world must add or refurbish 80 million km of power lines by 2040 and double grid investment to more than $600B/yr by 2030. The IEA *Energy and AI* report (2025-04) adds data-centre load doubling to ~945 TWh by 2030, and nothing (AI, substitute tech, or policy) can deliver power without the wires, so this clears the core ≥8 bar on necessity.
- **COPX (9).** PTI's report on the IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (2026-07-17) keeps copper in supply deficit through 2035, and the 2025 edition had sized the 2035 gap at ~30% (Argus). The −1 from band 10 is for aluminium substitution at high prices; copper's sibling funds score lower because they are steel-led (XME 6) or iron-ore-led (PICK 7).
- **URA/URNM/NLR (8).** WNA's World Nuclear Fuel Report 2025 (press statement 2025-09-05) has reactor requirements rising from 68,920 tU in 2025 to just over 150,000 tU in 2040 as capacity roughly doubles to 746 GWe. The score stops at 8 because nuclear competes with gas and renewables+storage for the same load; it is not irreplaceable.

## Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo

- **silver (SLV, SIL):** the memo calls this a pure "rotation (leveraged unwind)". On necessity, the industrial base is shrinking as well: PV silver is forecast −19% in 2026 from thrifting and copper substitution (Silver Institute, 2026-04-15). Silver is band 7 −1, not a structural-growth story.
- **base-metals:** the memo treats XME/PICK/COPX as one copper story ("copper near record"). Only COPX is copper-pure; XME's top holdings are steel and met coal (Schwab 2026-06-15) and PICK's are BHP/iron ore, so necessity splits 9 / 7 / 6, and only COPX clears the core ≥8 bar.
- **gold:** the memo implicitly treats GLD as a broad core theme. On necessity it scores 7, not ≥8: central-bank buying slowed to 863t in 2025 (WGC), and gold is a reserve preference with substitutes, not a physical need.
- **reit (DTCR):** the memo's "Equinix soft guide" framing could read as weakening demand. CBRE H1 2026 has North American vacancy at a record-low 1.4% (Construction Dive 2026-09-02), so necessity is 8, a broad theme at the ≥8 bar.
- **grid/infra:** the memo pairs GRID and PAVE. On necessity they split 9 vs 7, because PAVE is US construction that depends on IIJA funding, not the electrification build-out.
