Top-3 notes:
- GRID: The IEA's Electricity Mid-Year Update 2026 (July 2026) forecasts global electricity demand growth of 3.6% in 2026 and 3.8% in 2027, with renewables overtaking coal, and its Electricity 2026 work puts grid investment needing to rise about 50% to around $600bn a year by 2030 against 2,500 GW stuck in connection queues. That queue is the load-bearing fact: no amount of new generation meets demand if the wires cannot deliver it, which is why GRID scores 10 rather than merely 7.
- PAVE: The same IEA grid bottleneck supports broad infrastructure, but PAVE scores 9, not 10, because its construction and materials exposure is deferrable in a downturn in a way pure transmission is not. The distinction matters: PAVE owns the build-out cycle, GRID owns the irreplaceable delivery layer, so a fiscal or rate pause hits PAVE's necessity first.
- SOXX (standing for the 9-point semis group PSI/XSD/SOXX/SMH/DRAM): McKinsey's 2026-09-18 forecast raises the 2030 semiconductor market to $2.3tn at a 19% CAGR, driven by about $800bn of hyperscaler capex in 2026 and $1tn in 2027, and states there are no substitutes for memory. The load-bearing fact is that constraint, not just growth: supply cannot catch demand until well into the next decade, so chips remain a civilisation input even if AI capex pauses for a quarter.
