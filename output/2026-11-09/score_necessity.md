Top-3 notes:
- GRID (10): The load-bearing fact is that the grid itself is the bottleneck, not generation: the IEA's Electricity 2026 report (Feb 2026) finds more than 2,500 GW of projects stalled in connection queues worldwide and says meeting demand through 2030 requires annual grid investment to rise roughly 50% from about $400bn. No substitute exists for transmission — every electron from any source must cross wires, so this demand is structural and unavoidable.
- Nuclear — URA/NLR/URNM (9): The load-bearing fact is the World Nuclear Association's demand path: uranium requirements rise from about 68,900 tonnes in 2025 to more than 150,000 tonnes by 2040 as installed capacity grows from 398 GWe to 746 GWe, while mine production (~60kt) already trails reactor needs (~69kt). The AI leg is concrete, not aspirational: by mid-2026 every major hyperscaler had signed at least one nuclear deal, 13 projects totalling over 9.8 GW.
- Semis — PSI/DRAM/SOXX/XSD/SMH (9): The load-bearing fact is the IEA's October 2026 Powering AI report: data-centre electricity use was about 485 TWh in 2025 and is projected at roughly 950 TWh by 2030, with hyperscaler capex above $700bn in 2026. Compute is the substrate of that buildout, and no alternative technology routes around silicon — the necessity is structural even though the demand cycle is not.
