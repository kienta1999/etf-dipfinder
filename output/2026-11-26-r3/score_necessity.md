Top-3 notes:
- **COPX (9):** The load-bearing fact is the IEA's Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (released 2026-07-17): primary copper supply is projected ~25% short of requirements in 2035 under today's policies, while refined demand (~28 Mt in 2025, +3.7%) grows >25% by 2040. The gap narrowed from ~30% in the 2025 edition, which is why this is 9 and not 10, but no plausible scenario closes it (mining.com / gtreview reporting of the IEA, July 2026).
- **GRID (9):** The IEA's Electricity 2026 says annual grid investment must rise ~50% by 2030 from ~USD 400bn, and that transformer and cable prices and lead times have roughly doubled in four years, with large transformers taking up to 4 years. Wires have no substitute in any energy pathway: renewables, nuclear, gas and data centres all need them (iea.org Electricity 2026 Grids chapter and IEA transmission press release, 2026; McKinsey's 38% EU+NA transformer shortfall for 2025).
- **SMH (9):** WSTS's Spring 2026 forecast (2026-06-02, wsts.org) puts the chip market at ~$1.51tn in 2026 and ~$1.9tn in 2027 (+27%), with every region growing. The IEA's 2026 update keeps data-centre electricity doubling from 485 TWh (2025) to ~950 TWh (2030). The −1 from band 10 is for cyclicality: 2026's ~90% jump is inflated by memory pricing, and WSTS's Autumn 2026 update (due December 2026) has not been published yet.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- **Solar/clean:** the memo says the 2025 law "phases out renewable credits by 2028". The binding dates are tighter. The 45Y/48E begin-construction window closed 2026-07-04, and projects started after it must be placed in service by 2027-12-31 (K&L Gates 2025-07-23; Troutman). I score that as subsidy dependence on the US leg (TAN −1, PBW band 5), not a demand collapse: IEA Renewables 2026 still has 3.6 TW of solar in 2026-31.
- **Silver:** the memo frames SLV/SIL purely as a rates trade. On necessity, the World Silver Survey 2026 (2026-04-15) shows industrial demand *falling* for a second year (−3%, PV thrifting and substitution), so the "sixth deficit" is investment-led, not need-led. That caps silver at 6.
- **Nuclear:** the memo cites spot U3O8 "in the mid-$80s" as support. That is price, not necessity. The necessity case is WNA's 2040 requirement of >150,000 tU against ~60,000 tU of 2025 primary supply (primary, 2025-09-05). Because power is substitutable, uranium is band 7+1, not band 10.
- **Gold:** confirmed. The memo's 321 t central-bank figure matches the WGC primary page (Q3 2026, published 2026-11-03), and YTD is 675 t (+6%).
- **Crypto-equity:** agree with the memo that miners now trade on AI/HPC. For necessity that lifts WGMI to band 5 (>$70bn of HPC contracts), while BLOK stays crypto-adoption-dependent.
- **ai/robot:** agree with the memo that BOTZ/ROBO are less AI-capex-linked. IFR shows their demand is factory automation (+9% in 2026, Europe flat), so they get 7 against 8 for AIQ/BAI/AIPO.
