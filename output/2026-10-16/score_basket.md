Top-3 notes:
GLD is the cleanest basket in the field because it holds allocated physical gold and nothing else, so there is no constituent, credit or roll risk to dilute the theme. Its only cost is the 0.40% sponsor fee (SPDR Gold Shares / State Street, Oct 2026).
SLV earns the same purity logic for silver — 100% physical silver in a grantor trust — but pays 0.50%, ten basis points more than GLD for the same structure. That fee gap, not holdings quality, is the whole one-point difference (iShares Silver Trust, Oct 2026).
XAR and IGF tie at 9 for opposite constructions: XAR's equal weighting keeps every defense name near 5% at a 0.35% fee, while IGF caps global infrastructure names near 5.5% across 75 holdings at 0.39%. Both avoid the single-name and top-10 concentration that drags most thematic siblings to 7 or below (State Street XAR and iShares IGF, Oct 2026).

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- The dossier groups URNM, NLR and URA as one nuclear theme, but their baskets differ sharply: NLR (7, top name 9.8%) is a diversified nuclear chain, while URNM and URA (both 3) each put over 20% in Cameco alone.
- The dossier groups XAR, SHLD, ITA and PPA as one defense-US theme, but basket quality spans 9 (XAR, equal-weight) to 5 (ITA, top-10 ~75% with RTX ~17-22%).
- The dossier's ai/robot note cites BOTZ concentration (41.6% in top 5 in its source); current issuer data show top-5 nearer 42-48% and top-10 ~56%, and QTUM/BAI/AIPO are materially different baskets from BOTZ/ARKQ/ROBO, not one interchangeable theme basket.
- EUAD is far more concentrated than the dossier's "concentrated Rolls-Royce/Safran/Airbus" line implies: those three are ~59% and the top-10 are ~95% of the fund.
