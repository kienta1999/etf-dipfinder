## Top-3 notes

**Semis (SMH/SOXX/DRAM/PSI/XSD — 9):** The WSTS spring 2026 forecast puts the 2026 semiconductor market at $1.51 trillion, up ~90% year-on-year, with memory up ~250% to over $800 billion — the largest repricing of the industry in its history. That makes chips the closest thing in this panel to a no-substitute, civilisation-critical input, and the single point deducted is for concentration: the boom is AI/memory-led and SemiAnalysis has already cut AI-accelerator capex 28% for 2028 and 40% for 2030. (Sources: WSTS spring 2026 forecast via SIA; SemiAnalysis via dossier §C.)

**Nuclear (URA/URNM/NLR — 8):** The IEA's World Energy Outlook 2026 lifts global nuclear capacity from 420 GW in 2024 to 592 GW by 2035, with nearly 90 GW under construction as of mid-2026 — the highest buildout in over 35 years, across more than 50 countries. The one-point deduction from the top band is that nuclear competes with renewables-plus-storage for new generation, so its necessity is firm-baseload necessity rather than no-substitute necessity. (Source: IEA World Energy Outlook 2026, Nov 2026.)

**Defense (PPA/ITA/XAR/SHLD — 8):** SIPRI confirmed in April 2026 that global military spending hit a record $2.89 trillion in 2025, its 11th consecutive annual increase, and NATO's June 2025 Hague summit committed allies to 3.5% of GDP on core defence by 2035. The one-point deduction from the top band is that defence demand is ultimately policy-driven — a peace settlement or a change of government could reverse it, unlike a physical input the world cannot do without. (Source: SIPRI Apr 2026; NATO Hague Summit Jun 2025.)

## Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo

- **Solar (TAN, 7) vs memo "unclear":** the memo's caution leans on the 2026 additions dip and high rates, but IEA Renewables 2026 (Nov 6, 2026) still sees 3,605 GW of new solar in 2026–2031 — three of every four new renewable GW — and attributes the 2026 dip to China's switch to auction-based support, not a demand collapse. Necessity is a 5–10 year question, and on that horizon solar is structurally growing.
- **Brazil (EWZ, 5) vs memo "break":** Lula's runoff win erased the Bolsonaro-rally positioning, but that breaks a trade thesis, not Brazil's necessity as a large real commodity economy — its demand is cyclical with no structural growth claim, which is band 5, not a break in what the world needs from Brazil.
- **Crypto (WGMI/BLOK/ETHA/IBIT, 3–4) vs memo "unclear":** the memo treats crypto as an open question, but on this lens the demand is discretionary investment demand that rests on BTC holding its price and adoption — BTC at ~$56k is below JPMorgan's ~$86k average miner production cost and spot ETFs saw record outflows. That is band 3, not unclear.
- **Lithium (LIT, 7) vs memo "unclear":** the memo is right that carbonate oversupply (~$12.90/kg, Scotiabank seeing <$15/kg next year) is fundamental, but that is a price/cause problem, not a necessity problem — IEA sees ~23M EVs in 2026 rising past 40% of sales by 2030 plus fast storage growth, so the 5–10 year need for lithium stands at band 7.
- **Silver (SLV/SIL, 6):** agree with the memo's direction but one notch lower than a rotation-only read — the Silver Institute's Nov 12, 2026 figures show PV silver demand falling 19% in 2026 on thrifting and substitution, so the theme's biggest growth leg is actively shrinking even while the market stays in deficit.
