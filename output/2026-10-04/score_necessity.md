Top-3 notes:
- XLU: EIA's September STEO (press release 2026-09-09) expects US electricity sales at a record 4,135 BkWh in 2026 and 4,211 BkWh in 2027, about +2%/yr, led by data centers and manufacturing (commercial +3.3%). Globally, IEA Electricity 2026 has demand growing 3.6%/yr through 2030, about 50% faster than the prior decade, and data-centre use doubling from 485 to 950 TWh. Electricity has no substitute, so the only deduction is the behind-the-meter route-around.
- IGF: IEA World Energy Investment 2026 (launched 2026-05-28; Hanwha summary 2026-07-22) puts 2026 network spending at about \$550B, up nearly 20% y/y, in \$3.4T of total energy investment. Grids must grow over 20% in length by 2030. Regulated networks, pipelines and toll roads are natural monopolies that nothing routes around.
- URNM: the WNA World Nuclear Fuel Report 2025 (press statement 2025-09-05) has a reference case of 746 GWe by 2040, up from 372 GWe, with uranium requirements rising from 68,920 tU to just over 150,000 tU (lower case 107,000 tU, still +55%). Mines face depletion in the mid-2030s. Even the low case is structural growth, but gas and renewables+storage are real substitutes, which keeps it at band 7+1 rather than 10.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Silver: the memo blames the fall on the yield high hitting "both monetary and industrial cases". On necessity, the industrial case is eroding for its own reasons. The Silver Institute 2026 survey has PV silver -19% from thrifting/substitution and total industrial demand -3%, which is why SLV ranks below gold here.
- Solar/clean: the memo leans "break" on policy. That is a US-subsidy fact, not a global necessity fact. IEA still has solar at ~77-80% of 2025-30 renewable additions (3,546 GW) despite cutting the US solar forecast ~40%. Only PBW's hydrogen/fuel-cell slice looks subsidy-dependent, hence PBW 6 vs ICLN/TAN 7.
- Nuclear: the memo makes AI-power demand the thesis question. Necessity rests on a broader base, WNA's government-target reference case (capacity doubling by 2040), so an AI-capex wobble does not move the 5-10y need.
- Defense: the memo says no budget cut was cited. True for now, but SIPRI (April 2026) shows US spending actually fell in 2025 while Europe rose 14%. US growth resumes only if the ~\$1.15T FY2027 NDAA and appropriations land, and both are still unfinished under CRs.
- Grid/infra: the memo treats IGF and PAVE as one rate de-rating. They are different themes, global network operators vs US construction suppliers. PAVE's federal funding base (IIJA) expired 2026-09-30 with reauthorization only through House committee, a funding-dependence the memo does not mention.
