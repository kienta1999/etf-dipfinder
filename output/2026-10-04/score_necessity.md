Top-3 notes: (1) IGF: the single load-bearing fact is that no generator — solar, nuclear, fusion — reaches a load without wires, so nothing (AI, batteries, policy) routes around the grid theme. IEA's 2026 grid work says global grid investment must double to more than $600B per year by 2030, which makes the structural demand claim a dated institutional number, not a vibe. Source: IEA 2026 grid report (via UN/IEA, Sep 2026). (2) PAVE: the same grid thesis in US-domestic form — the EIA projects a record 86 GW of US utility-scale capacity additions in 2026, with solar (51%), batteries (28%) and wind (14%) dominating, and every one of those megawatts needs transmission buildout to clear the 2,500+ GW global grid-connection queue. That makes PAVE's necessity a 10 even though its dip is shallow (dd −10%, rr 1.5). Source: EIA via enerdata.net, 2026-02-24. (3) XLU: electricity itself is the civilisation-critical input with no substitute, and the structural demand story has re-accelerated — the IEA projects ~3.6% annual global electricity demand growth, with renewables set to surpass coal as the largest generation source in 2026 after near-parity in 2025. XLU's −17.7% derating is a rates story (10y at ~5.2–5.35%), not a demand story. Source: IEA Electricity report via solarquarter.com, 2026-02-11.
