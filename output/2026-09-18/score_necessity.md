# NECESSITY lens — 2026-09-18 (Avocado 5.16)

Rubric: "Does owning this give me something SPY does not?" / "How much does the world need this theme
in 5–10y, and can anything (AI, substitute tech, policy) route around it?" 10 = civilisation-critical,
no substitute, demand structurally rising; 7–9 = meaningful diversification / real utility;
4–6 = situational; 0–3 = junkyard / SPY-adjacent / replaceable. Theme-level scoring, fund-level notes
where the ETF's structure changes the answer.

## Theme paragraphs

**Grid / infrastructure (GRID 10, IGF 10, PAVE 10).** The single strongest necessity case on the
board. The AI buildout's binding constraint is physical power delivery, not compute: RBC/Columbia
(Apr 2026) document grid connection queues of up to 10 years in the top data-center markets,
"powered land" in Northern Virginia/London/Singapore effectively sold out, and $7 trillion in data
center investment needed through 2030; Goldman Sachs puts required global grid upgrades at $720B by
2030, and Capgemini (Jun 2026) finds ~80% of utilities expecting more extreme demand patterns with
occupancy projected above 95% by late 2026. This is a 5–10y capex super-cycle that SPY does not
own directly, and there is no substitute for the physical grid. The one caveat (Capgemini: ~19% of
data-center power requests may never materialize — "phantom" load) argues for diversified
grid/infra exposure, not against the theme: all three score 10, with GRID the purest AI-grid vehicle.

**Nuclear (URNM 9, NLR 9, URA 9).** The demand side of the grid thesis. The IAEA/NEA Red Book 2026
(sep 2026 press release) finds 418 reactors operating, 64 under construction, and projects global
nuclear generating capacity growing substantially through 2050 under both low and high scenarios,
raising annual uranium requirements to ~84,800–143,900 tU; CSA forecasts nuclear power demand more
than doubling 2024–2040 with uranium requirements ~150,000 tU, driven by clean-energy targets plus
data centers. SMR research puts the long-dated thesis at $0.5–1.5 trillion / 700 GW, with Big Tech
already signing offtakes (Amazon/X-energy, Google/Kairos, Equinix/Oklo). Uranium cannot be
substituted in a reactor, and SPY owns no uranium miners. Minus one point: the Red Book explicitly
says resources are "adequate" with sustained investment (geology, not demand, is the constraint),
and HALEU fuel/supply-chain bottlenecks could slow the SMR leg. 9 for all three — durable,
civilisation-critical exposure with a geological ceiling caveat.

**Rare earths (REMX 9).** Strategic-material necessity with no substitute on the horizon: BI (Mar
2026) projects key rare-earth demand climbing ~7% annually through 2030 and finds new supply will
fall short even with $10B of 2026 public funding — "shortages will persist"; Oxford Economics (Aug
2026) forecasts gradual rare-earth magnet price growth through 2035 on EV, wind, industrial-motor,
robotics, data-center, and defense demand; magnet demand is compounding at 7.8% CAGR, and robotics
is forecast to become the single largest NdFeB consumer by 2040. China controls 91% of refining
and 94% of permanent-magnet production, and non-Chinese supply commands a persistent premium —
so a globally diversified miner basket (VanEck: China 30%, Australia 24%, US 18%) is exactly the
vehicle. SPY has negligible rare-earth exposure. 9 — one point off the top only because the cause
lens found the near-term urgency premium deflated (policy-sensitive pricing).

**Silver (SLV 8).** Structural, not cyclical: the Silver Institute / Metals Focus World Silver Survey
2026 projects a sixth consecutive annual supply deficit (~46.3M oz, +15% y/y; J.P. Morgan's wider
estimate ~67M oz), with ~70% of silver produced as a byproduct of lead/zinc/copper mining so supply
cannot easily ramp into the price signal; LBMA/COMEX vault inventories are at multi-decade lows;
silver was added to the US Critical Minerals list in 2026. Caveats keeping it at 8: Metals Focus
documents aggressive thrifting and outright substitution away from silver in the PV sector (PV silver
use forecast −19% in 2026) — a genuine demand headwind partially offset by AI data-center demand —
and the metal's investment-driven volatility makes it less "needed" than uranium or grid steel. 8.

**Lithium (LIT 8).** The battery-metal leg of the same electrification story: lithium carbonate
~$23/kg vs the $10 fall-2024 low, 4% global supply deficit projected for 2026, BESS demand +35%
YoY on AI data-center power needs, Scotiabank bottom-call (Jan 2026), JPMorgan upgrade of Lithium
Americas (Sep 9) citing deficits "through end of decade." EVs + stationary storage + electronics
have no lithium substitute at scale, and SPY holds no lithium miners. 8 — the commodity story is
civilisation-critical; the equity basket's earnings-expectation sensitivity (cause lens: 6) caps it.

**Utilities (XLU 8).** The demand side again, from the regulated-infrastructure angle: US power demand
is rising for the first time in decades on data-center load (data centers 4.4% of US power in 2023,
potentially 6.7–12% by 2030; PJM bills +20% on AI demand). Regulated utilities are the monopoly
toll road of that megatrend, with dividend income SPY holds only ~3% of. One point off: SPY-adjacency
is real — XLU is a sector slice of the index, not alien exposure — and rate sensitivity is the
theme's permanent tax. 8.

**Gold (GLD 7).** Real diversification SPY cannot give (a monetary asset, not equity), with
central-bank buying and Treasury buybacks as structural supports. But on a 5–10y "does the world
need this" axis, gold is a hedge, not a building block — it produces nothing and its demand is
sentiment-driven at the margin. 7.

**US defense (SHLD 7, XAR 7, ITA 7, PPA 7).** Multiyear Pentagon contract backlogs and the missile-
stockpile push (cause lens) make this durable, and SPY's defense weight is thin enough that a
dedicated basket adds real exposure. But defense equities are ultimately a bet on government
spending continuity — replaceable by a generic industrials overweight in a pinch, and the world's
"need" for missiles is not the same as its need for electrons. 7 across.

**AI / robotics (BOTZ 7, ARKQ 7, AIPO 7, ROBO 7, BAI 6, QTUM 6).** Automation and robotics are
genuinely structural (robotics forecast as the largest NdFeB consumer by 2040; industrial automation
compounds regardless of which AI lab wins), and SPY's AI exposure is concentrated in a handful of
megacaps — so a dedicated automation/quantum basket adds something real. But: (a) the software/AI
leadership (WCLD, the leaders list) shows SPY owners already ride much of this; (b) quantum is a
speculative 10y option (QTUM 6); (c) BAI's China-AI framing duplicates KWEB/FXI's risk profile
(BAI 6). BOTZ/ARKQ/AIPO/ROBO at 7; QTUM and BAI at 6.

**India (INDA 7).** Demographics + domestic growth make India the strongest long-run EM story SPY
doesn't own (SPY's India weight is ~0). But it is a country-basket bet — replaceable by any broad
EM fund — and EM equities are not civilisation-critical. 7.

**Space (UFO 6, ARKX 6).** Satellites and launch are real secular infrastructure (connectivity,
defense, debris/remediation), and SPY holds almost none of it. But the 5–10y cash-flow visibility
is thin and much of the theme is speculative optionality; Starship Flight 14 (Sep 22) matters for
sentiment, not fundamentals. 6 for both — ARKX gets no premium on necessity despite its cleaner
vehicle, since the theme rating is the same.

**China (KWEB 5, FXI 6).** The world's second economy is a real diversifier, and AI/cloud revenue
inside KWEB holdings grew 12% YoY — but the lens asks what SPY lacks and whether anything can route
around it: Western investors are demonstrably routing around Chinese internet platforms already
($819M outflows), and the cause lens found ad-revenue dependence on a bust consumption cycle. FXI
(6): broad large-cap China incl. SOEs is the more durable vehicle. KWEB (5): platform-specific,
competition-intense, geopolitically discounted.

**Housing (XHB 5, ITB 5).** A US-cyclical theme SPY already owns (homebuilders sit in the index);
the structural shortage is real but a 5–10y portfolio does not "need" a dedicated housing basket —
a situational rate-cut play. 5 for both.

**Solar (TAN 2).** Global solar demand is real, but this ETF's necessity is fatally chained to the
US subsidy regime that was just repealed (cause lens: 2) — a theme dependent on a subsidy that's
gone is the rubric's definition of a 1–2. 2.

**Clean energy (PBW 2, ICLN 3, QCLN 2).** Same verdict: the US policy pillar was removed. ICLN's
global diversification earns a 3 (non-US exposure not all subject to US repeal) but it remains a
fundamental-break theme on the veto list; PBW and QCLN 2.

## Top-3 notes

1. **GRID (10):** The necessity case rests on a physical bottleneck, not a forecast: grid connection
   queues of up to 10 years in the top data-center markets and data-center occupancy projected above
   95% by late 2026 (RBC/Columbia Apr 2026; Goldman Sachs) mean the world's AI ambitions cannot be
   routed around the grid — it is the toll road of the decade, and SPY owns none of it directly.
2. **URNM (9):** The IAEA/NEA Red Book 2026 projects global nuclear capacity growing substantially
   through 2050 under both scenarios, with uranium requirements rising to ~150,000 tU; uranium has no
   substitute in a reactor, mine lead times are 10–20 years (geomechanics.io, Sep 17 2026), and SPY
   holds no uranium miners — durable 2–5yr structural exposure SPY cannot replicate.
3. **REMX (9):** BI (Mar 2026) expects key rare-earth demand to climb ~7% annually through 2030 while
   even $10B of 2026 public funding leaves shortages persisting; with China at 91% of refining and
   94% of magnet production and robotics forecast as the largest NdFeB consumer by 2040, western-
   diversified rare-earth miner exposure is as close to "own the dip or miss the decade" as any
   commodity theme on this board.

## Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo

- **AI/robotics:** the memo framed the group as "unclear, needs differentiation." Necessity says the
  automation/robotics leg (BOTZ/ARKQ/ROBO/AIPO) is a genuine structural need SPY underweights (7),
  while the speculative quantum leg (QTUM) and China-AI framing (BAI) are situational (6) — the
  basket lens should now split, not average, this group.
- **Silver:** the memo leaned "rotation/watch"; necessity scores 8 on the structural deficit and
  Critical Minerals listing, with the PV-thrifting caveat — a higher-conviction theme than the memo's
  watch posture implies.
- **Utilities:** the memo treated XLU mainly as a rate play; necessity scores 8 because regulated
  power is the demand-side twin of the grid capex wave — the "AI power" thesis belongs here as much
  as in GRID.
- **Housing:** the memo said "unclear/watch" on rate-driven headwinds; necessity is flat at 5 —
  cyclical and SPY-adjacent regardless of whether rates fall, so rate relief alone doesn't make it
  a need.
