Top-3 notes:
- **XLU (9):** The IEA's *Electricity 2026* executive summary (read from a mirrored copy of the IEA PDF; iea.org returns 403) forecasts global electricity demand rising 3.6%/yr over 2026-30, "50% higher" than the prior decade's average. The EIA STEO of 2026-10-06 (eia.gov, fetched) shows US Q3-2026 consumption +4% y/y with generation reaching 4,613 TWh in 2027. The only point off the 10 band is that residential demand is flat and the growth rests on commercial/data-centre load.
- **URNM (8):** The WNA World Nuclear Fuel Report (press statement 2025-09-05, fetched) has reactor uranium needs rising from ~68,920 tU in 2025 to just over 150,000 tU by 2040 as capacity nearly doubles to 746 GWe. Supply from existing mines falls after 2030 and new mines take 10-20 years. Gas and renewables plus storage are visible substitutes for the electrons, which is why it is 8 and not 10.
- **NLR (8):** This is the same WNA 2025-09-05 capacity path (398 to 746 GWe by 2040), applied to the operators and reactor builders NLR holds rather than miners. It is copied from the theme, with no sibling difference on this lens.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- **rare-earth:** Section C asks whether China's export-control suspension (to 2027-01-10) shrinks the scarcity premium as a permanent re-rating. On necessity it does not: the suspension is temporary, China still makes ~95% of magnets (IEA 2026-04-08), and DFARS 252.225-7052 legally mandates ex-China magnet supply for US defence from 2027-01-01.
- **silver:** Section C's "deflation from a parabolic peak may be a permanent re-rating" has a necessity basis. The Silver Institute's 2026 forecast of PV silver -19% from thrifting is substitution in progress, so silver scores below the other hard-asset themes (6, not 7).
- **crypto-equity:** Section C calls it rotation, "not a crypto-demand collapse". That holds for spot crypto, but the miners' own business is impaired: a record-low hashprice (~$27.7/PH/day, Jun 2026) left cash cost above the BTC price. Only WGMI's AI-hosting pivot and BLOK's stablecoin/exchange slice have any structural claim.
- **clean:** Section C marks it "unverified". Ember's primary review (2026-04-21) shows clean power met all 2025 global demand growth (+887 TWh vs +849 TWh). The weak spot is the US: the IEA cut its US outlook ~50% after the OBBBA, which is why PBW/QCLN (6) sit below ICLN (7).
- **ai/robot:** Section C has "no fund or theme item from the last 6 weeks". IFR World Robotics 2026 (2026-09-24, fetched) reports record installations of 603,307 (+11%) and a 7% CAGR to 2029.
- **reit:** Section C calls DTCR's AI-capex exposure "unverified". The IEA's *Electricity 2026* states data centres are ~50% of US demand growth to 2030, which is the need DTCR owns (holdings weight is the basket lens's question, not this one).
- **india:** Section C treats INDA as pure rates/FPI rotation. On necessity there is a separate, non-rotation flag: the IT-services slice is a business AI routes around (Nifty IT at a 3-year low in 2026 on AI fears; CRISIL sees 1-3% sector growth).
- **space:** Section C's "SpaceX listed and down ~36%" is a price fact, not a need fact. The WEF/McKinsey $1.8T-by-2035 path (Apr 2024) is unchanged, so the theme scores 7.
