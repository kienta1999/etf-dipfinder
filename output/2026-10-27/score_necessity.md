Top-3 notes:
- GRID (10): The single load-bearing fact is the IEA's finding that reaching national goals means adding or refurbishing over 80 million kilometres of grid by 2040 — the equivalent of the entire existing global grid — with annual grid investment needing to nearly double to over $600 billion by 2030. There is no substitute technology for moving electricity from generator to user, so this is the only theme scored at the civilisation-critical band with no deduction. Source: IEA, *Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions*, executive summary, fetched 2026-10-27.
- COPX (9): The load-bearing fact is the IEA's projection that the current copper mine pipeline covers only about 70% of 2035 demand — a ~30% shortfall — while copper demand rises 50% by 2040 in the Net Zero scenario, driven by grids, EVs and data centres. The one-point deduction from band 10 is aluminium substitution in some conductors, which is real but partial. Source: IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025, reported by Argus on 2025-05-21.
- PAVE (9): The load-bearing fact is ASCE's estimate of a $3.7 trillion US infrastructure investment gap through 2033 ($9.1T needed for a state of good repair vs ~$5.4T projected funding) in its 2025 Report Card. Roads, water and bridges have no substitute, but the basket also carries discretionary construction exposure, hence band 10 minus one rather than GRID's clean 10. Source: ASCE, 2025 Report Card for America's Infrastructure.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- The memo calls gold (GLD/GDXJ) "rotation" on central-bank buying (289t in Q2, 14 weeks of ETF inflows); on necessity that bid is reserve diversification, not civilisation need — gold scores 6, not a high-necessity core on this lens, whatever its cause score.
- The memo calls crypto-equity "rotation" because spot IBIT leads; necessity disagrees in kind, not degree — miners/blockchain equity score 2-3 because the underlying service is substitutable, so a flow reversal would not make the theme needed.
- The memo leans "break-watch" on lithium and silver on price/surplus and thrifting evidence; those are cause facts. On necessity both stay at band 7 — lithium demand still rises >3x by 2040 (IEA stated policies) and silver industrial demand was 657.4 Moz in 2025 — with thrifting acting as the band-7 substitution cap, not a break.
- The memo's "rotation" label for space understates nothing on this lens but implies more safety than necessity supports: space is band 7 (structurally growing, partly substitutable by terrestrial networks), not a critical-need theme.
