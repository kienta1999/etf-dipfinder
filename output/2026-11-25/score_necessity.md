Top-3 notes: Semis (SMH, 10): there is no substitute for silicon compute — every AI model, data center and robot runs on it, and the demand signal is the capex itself, with JPMorgan putting 2026 AI capex at $900bn and $5.5tn cumulative by 2030. The dossier's own evidence corroborates the structural claim: semis' earnings more than doubled in a year even as the group sold off, so the drop is a valuation reset on a need that keeps growing.
Grid (GRID, 10): the grid is the one input every other theme needs — the IEA's Electricity 2026 report has global electricity demand growing 3.6% a year through 2030, 50% faster than the prior decade's average, with over 2,500 GW of projects stalled in connection queues. The IEA puts the fix at grid investment rising ~50% from ~$400bn a year, which is a structural, policy-backed spending need no substitute technology can route around.
Nuclear (URA, 8): nuclear is the only scalable firm low-carbon power source, and the IEA records nuclear generation hitting an all-time high in 2025 with growth set to more than double its 2021–25 pace (+2.8% a year to 2030) and 70+ GW under construction. It scores 8, not 10, because gas and renewables-plus-storage can substitute for the same firm-power role — but the dossier's spot uranium at ~$90/lb, up ~10% YTD while equities collapsed, shows the fuel demand never broke.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo: none — the memo's buckets are cause judgments (rotation vs break vs unclear), not necessity claims, and nothing in them contradicts these necessity scores; in particular the memo's "unclear" on lithium and clean energy reflects price/policy headwinds, while their 5–10y necessity (band 7) rests on EV sales above 20m in 2025 and solar/wind at ~$40/MWh, which the memo does not dispute.
