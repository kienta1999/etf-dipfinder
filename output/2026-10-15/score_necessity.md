Top-3 notes:
- XLU: The load-bearing fact is EIA's record-demand path — US consumption rising from 4,195bn kWh in 2025 to 4,288bn in 2026 and 4,356bn in 2027 on data centres and electrification (Reuters/EIA STEO, 2026-10-06). Electricity delivery has no substitute technology, so this is a Band 10 score, not a rate-cycle judgement.
- GRID: The load-bearing fact is the IEA's grid stocktake — 80 million km of lines to add or replace by 2040, equal to the entire existing grid, with annual investment doubling to over $600bn by 2030 (IEA Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions). No generation technology routes around transmission and distribution, which is why the pure grid fund scores 10 while the diluted infra funds score 9.
- IGF: The load-bearing fact is the same IEA grid requirement, cross-checked against EIA's record 2026-27 US power demand (Reuters/EIA, 2026-10-06). IGF scores 9, one below GRID, only because its basket holds broader infrastructure beyond the grid itself — a sibling difference in purity, not in the underlying need.

Disagreements with dossier:
- Solar/clean: the dossier leans 'break' for the US residential sleeve (credit phase-out, installs -21% in 2026) — that is a cause/basket fact about one sleeve; on necessity, global solar is still Band 7 (IEA: ~80% of renewables growth to 2030, lowest-cost), so TAN scores 7 and only the broader, more subsidy-dependent clean funds score 6.
- Crypto-equity: the dossier calls the dip a rotation (a cause judgement) — on necessity the theme is low regardless of cause, because proof-of-work is directly substitutable (proof-of-stake) and enterprise blockchain is substitutable by conventional rails, hence WGMI/BKCH 2 and BLOK 3.
- Space: the dossier is 'unclear' on cause — on necessity the $1.8T-by-2035 WEF/McKinsey path is real growth, but terrestrial fibre/PNT alternatives cap it at Band 7, scored 6.
