Top-3 notes:
- GRID: The single load-bearing fact is the IEA's Electricity 2026 finding (released Feb 6, 2026) that annual grid investment must rise ~50% by 2030 from ~$400B/year, with over 2,500 GW of projects stuck in connection queues — the grid is the bottleneck of the entire electrification story, and no substitute delivery system exists. That queue figure is what lifts grid/infra from "structurally growing" to civilisation-critical: without the wires, none of the AI, EV, or renewables demand growth can be delivered.
- PSI: The single load-bearing fact is McKinsey's 2026 revision of the semiconductor market to a $2.3T base case by 2030 (from $1.6T in autumn 2025), driven by hyperscaler AI capex of ~$800B in 2026 rising to ~$1T in 2027. Chips sit inside every AI, EV, grid, and defence system in this ballot, so their necessity is upstream of nearly every other theme — and no device class has a non-silicon alternative at scale.
- XSD: The same McKinsey $2.3T-by-2030 semiconductor outlook (2026) carries this rank, with the added point that wafer shipment growth (~7% CAGR) is not keeping pace with value growth — meaning the constraint is physical supply of advanced chips, not demand. That supply-constrained, structurally rising profile is exactly the 10-band: the world cannot route around semiconductors in 5–10 years, it can only build more fabs.
