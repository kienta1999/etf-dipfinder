Top-3 notes:
- **GRID (9):** NERC's 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment (released 2026-01-29, via UtilityDive, fetched) projects US summer peak demand up 224 GW (+24%) over ten years. That is the fastest growth since tracking began in 1995, and data centres drive most of it. IEA Electricity 2026 (2026-02-07, via Down To Earth, fetched; iea.org returned 403) adds global demand at +3.6%/yr to 2030 and grid investment rising ~50% to ~$600bn/yr, so a Texas approvals pause shifts where wires get built, not whether.
- **COPX (9):** S&P Global's primary release of 2026-01-08 (fetched) projects copper demand rising 50% to 42 Mt by 2040 and "a supply deficit of 10 million metric tons by 2040—25% below projected demand". AI adds only ~2 Mt of that, so the need rests on broad electrification and defence rather than one fragile driver.
- **SMH/SOXX/PSI/XSD (9):** WSTS's Spring 2026 release (2026-06-02, PDF fetched) forecasts the chip market +90% to $1.51tn in 2026 and a further +27% to ~$1.9tn in 2027, with logic +27%. Nothing substitutes for semiconductors; the 1-point haircut reflects that 2026's surge is "overwhelmingly" memory pricing (+250%), the same fact that holds DRAM at 8.

Disagreements with the dossier's first-pass memo:
- Section B/C calls the selloff "one cluster, one cause... not 20 separate problems". On necessity the themes are far apart, with copper/grid/semis at 9 and crypto at 3, so a common price driver must not be read as a common thesis.
- Silver (C: "solar silver demand -19%" filed as a cause): the Silver Institute attributes it to thrifting and copper substitution in cells, which is a structural route-around. That caps silver at band 5 rather than making it a cyclical blip.
- Rare earth (C: "US-China thaw pricing"): the thaw does not reduce necessity. China still refines 85% (IEA 2026-07-17), and planned ex-China magnet output covers only a third by 2035.
- Grid/infra (C: the Texas moratorium as the driver): NERC's +224 GW ten-year forecast and CBRE's 1.4% vacancy (2026-09-08) say the need is intact, and the pledge covers new approvals only.
- Regime (C: "fears of slower AI/data-center buildout"): CBRE has more than 80% of 7,481 MW under construction preleased (Sep 2026), so on this lens AI/DTCR stay at 8.
- Semis (C: "DRAM revenue +369% y/y" read as fundamentals beating sentiment): for necessity that spike is price-driven (WSTS: memory +250%), which is why DRAM scores one point below the broad semis funds, not above.
- Clean/solar (C: "tax-credit uncertainty"): the OBBBA dates are fixed, not uncertain (begin construction by 2026-07-04 or in service by 2027-12-31). The US subsidy loss is known and global solar demand (IEA, ~80% of additions) does not rest on it, so clean/solar stay at band 7 rather than 1-3.
- Defence (C: Democratic Congress means a smaller Pentagon budget): that is a US budget-level point. SIPRI shows world spending up for an 11th straight year and Europe +14%, so the US funds hold band 7 and only global SHLD gets the +1.
