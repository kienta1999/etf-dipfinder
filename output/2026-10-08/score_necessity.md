Top-3 notes:
- IGF: The IEA's Electricity 2026 report (reported Feb 2026) says annual grid investment must rise ~50% — from roughly $400B to ~$600B — by 2030, and counts more than 2,500 GW of generation, storage and data-center projects stuck in global connection queues; grid build takes 5–15 years versus 1–3 for data centers, making the network the decade's binding constraint. Nothing routes around it: more solar, more gas, more AI load all need the same wires.
- XLU: EIA's September 2026 STEO (covered by Reuters, 2026-10-06) puts US electricity demand at back-to-back records — 4,288 billion kWh in 2026 and 4,356 in 2027 — driven by AI data centers and electrification, with data-center power demand projected at 132 GW rising to 290 GW by 2030. Regulated utilities sell the one good every scenario needs, and the sector's dip is discount-rate repricing (10y >5.3% vs ~3% yields), not earnings impairment — which is cause's problem, not necessity's.
- REMX: IEA's Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 projects rare-earth demand to rise 50–90% by 2040 under stated policies while China holds ~85% of magnet rare-earth separation — a concentration no Western program dents before ~2035 (Reuters, 2026-09-14). The kicker is legal, not cyclical: DFARS 252.225-7052 (acquisition.gov, fetched 2026-10-05) bars US defense contractors from delivering China-origin NdFeB/SmCo magnets from 2027-01-01, mandating a parallel ex-China supply chain regardless of prices.
